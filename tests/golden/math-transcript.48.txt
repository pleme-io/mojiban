Sure! Here's a neat little calculus exercise and
its solution:

Problem:
Evaluate the definite integral
    ∫₀¹ ln(1+x) dx.

Step-by-step Solution

1. Find an antiderivative of ln(1+x).
   Use integration by parts:
   ∫ ln(1+x) dx = (1+x)ln(1+x) − ∫ (1+x)/(1+x)
     dx = (1+x)ln(1+x) − x + C.

2. Apply the limits x=0 to x=1.
       F(1) = (1+1)ln(1+1) − 1
            = 2ln 2 − 1,
       F(0) = (1+0)ln(1+0) − 0 = 1⋅ ln 1 = 0.

3. Subtract:
       ∫₀¹ ln(1+x) dx = F(1)−F(0) = 2ln 2 − 1.

Result
    [∫₀¹ ln(1+x) dx = 2ln 2 − 1 ≈ 0.386294]

So the area under the curve y=ln(1+x) from x=0
to x=1 is 2ln 2 − 1. If you'd like to see
